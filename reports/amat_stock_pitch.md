# Applied Materials Stock Pitch

**Status: Watchlist research memo**

This memo is an educational equity-research exercise based on public company filings. It is not investment advice and intentionally does not present a price target until the valuation model has current peer and consensus inputs.

## View

Applied Materials merits close research because its equipment portfolio spans process steps that become harder as chips add 3D structures, advanced packaging, and increasingly demanding process-control requirements. The present view is **Watchlist**, not Long or Short. The operating evidence is strong, but a defensible investment conclusion requires an explicit valuation against the current share price and cycle-normalised earnings.

## Evidence to start from

- Fiscal 2025 revenue was $28.4bn. Semiconductor Systems represented $20.8bn, Applied Global Services $6.4bn, and Corporate and Other $1.2bn.
- Fiscal 2025 cash from operations was $8.0bn and capital expenditure was $2.3bn. This provides a transparent starting point for free-cash-flow analysis.
- In Q3 FY2026, Applied reported record revenue of $9.12bn, GAAP gross margin of 50.3%, GAAP operating income of $3.08bn, and GAAP EPS of $3.17.
- Management reported that semiconductor-equipment spending serves foundry, logic, DRAM, NAND, and advanced packaging demand. The 2025 10-K explicitly identifies AI/data-centre demand, complex architectures, and technology transitions as drivers, but it also calls out volatility, customer concentration, export controls, trade policy, and the cyclicality of customer capex as risks.

Sources: Applied Materials Q3 FY2026 results, 13 August 2026; Applied Materials FY2025 10-K, filed 12 December 2025. Links are included in the PDF memo and `learning/resources.md`.

## Three research questions

1. How much of recent demand reflects durable process-complexity growth rather than a cyclical capacity peak?
2. Can services and the installed base reduce earnings volatility as Semiconductor Systems shifts with customer capex?
3. What does the current share price imply about revenue growth, operating margin, and free-cash-flow conversion?

## Catalysts to test

- Continued spending on advanced logic, high-bandwidth memory, and advanced packaging.
- Share gains or stronger mix in process-control, packaging, or materials-engineering steps.
- Services revenue and contract renewal expanding faster than equipment revenue during a downcycle.

## Disconfirming risks

- A memory or foundry capex correction reduces tool demand faster than services can offset it.
- Export restrictions or trade-policy changes limit access to customers or reduce competitiveness.
- Customer concentration, technology substitution, or a lower-than-expected return on new capacity weakens the long-term thesis.

## Valuation work still required

Build a five-year DCF from fiscal 2025 free cash flow, then compare implied EV/EBIT and P/E against a peer set that includes ASML, Lam Research, KLA, and Tokyo Electron. Use a range of cycle-normalised revenue growth, margin, WACC, and terminal-growth assumptions. The goal is to identify what the market price already assumes, not to manufacture a target price.
